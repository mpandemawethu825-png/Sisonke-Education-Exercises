Arithmetic Sequences

A sequence where you add a constant number to get the next term.

General Formula:
\( T_n = a + (n - 1)d \)
a = first term, d = difference
⚠️
The Test: To prove a sequence is arithmetic, show that:
\( T_2 - T_1 = T_3 - T_2 \).

Example: Determine the 50th term of: 3 ; 7 ; 11 ; ...

1. Identify variables: \(a = 3\), \(d = 4\)

2. Substitute: \( T_{50} = 3 + (49)(4) \)

3. Calculate: \( T_{50} = 199 \)

Geometric Sequences

A sequence where you multiply by a constant number (ratio) to get the next term.

General Formula:
\( T_n = a \cdot r^{n-1} \)
r = constant ratio
⚠️
The Test: To prove a sequence is geometric, show that:
\( \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{T_3}{T_2} \).

Question: Find \(T_n\) for: 2 ; 6 ; 18 ; ...

\(a = 2\), \(r = 3\)

Answer: \( T_n = 2 \cdot (3)^{n-1} \)

Sigma Notation (Σ)

Sigma is a Greek symbol used to represent the sum of a series.

\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} T_k \)
Sum from term k=1 up to term n

Example: Expand and solve \( \sum_{k=1}^{3} (2k) \)

Term 1 (k=1): \(2(1) = 2\)

Term 2 (k=2): \(2(2) = 4\)

Term 3 (k=3): \(2(3) = 6\)

Total: \( 2 + 4 + 6 = 12 \)

Sum of Arithmetic Series

\( S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] \)
Use when you don't know the last term
\( S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l) \)
Use when you know the last term (l)
📝
Exam Proof: You are often asked to prove the formula \( S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] \).

1. Write sum forwards: \(S_n = a + (a+d) + ... + (l-d) + l\)

2. Write sum backwards: \(S_n = l + (l-d) + ... + (a+d) + a\)

3. Add equations: \(2S_n = (a+l) + (a+l) ... \)

4. There are n terms: \(2S_n = n(a+l)\)

5. Substitute \(l\): \(S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]\)

Sum of Geometric Series

\( S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} \)
Use when r > 1
\( S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r} \)
Use when r < 1

Question: Sum of 8 terms: 3 + 6 + 12 + ...

\(a=3, r=2, n=8\)

\(S_8 = \frac{3(2^8 - 1)}{1} = 3(255) = 765\)

Sum to Infinity

A geometric series can only be summed to infinity if the terms get smaller and smaller. This is called "Converging".

🛑
Condition for Convergence:
The ratio \(r\) must be between -1 and 1.
\( -1 < r < 1 \).
Formula:
\( S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r} \)

Example: Calculate \( 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + ... \)

1. Check ratio: \(r = \frac{1}{2}\). Since \(-1 < 0.5 < 1\), it converges.

2. Formula: \(S_\infty = \frac{1}{1 - 0.5}\)

3. Solve: \( \frac{1}{0.5} = 2 \)
